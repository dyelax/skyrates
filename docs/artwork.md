# Art direction

Skyrates uses original raster scenes, portraits, and sprites generated with the built-in ImageGen tool. The game serves the finished WebP assets from [public/art](../public/art/). Navigation icons use Lucide. The compass, targeting arcs, route lines, and instruments are interface graphics.

## Shared direction

Premium original game art for SKYRATES, an earnest solarpunk aetherpunk trading RPG. High-density atmospheric pixel art with sophisticated pixel realism, finely placed visible pixels and clustered dithering, believable scale and architecture, beautiful restrained warm light and cool petrol/teal/violet shadows. Not vector, not low-poly, not cartoon, not smooth digital brushwork, not isometric mobile game icons. No text, labels, logos, glyphs, frames, UI, or watermark.

## Explorable quays

Single seamless wide landscape 16:9 establishing background for a luxurious point-and-click graphic adventure game. It is an ACTUAL explorable outdoor port plaza, not a poster: five interactive destinations are physically present, LARGE, clearly separated and grounded in one uninterrupted scene with visible paths between them. Eye-level slightly elevated panoramic perspective like looking from an upstairs landing, looking downward about 10 degrees. Environment is the star, lived in with people doing work, material craft, sunlight, volumetric atmosphere and complex pixel detail. Arrange all five places in middle 65% of the image height with most sky/background silhouettes across top 22%. Leave a little negative space near top left for title HUD and keep very bottom mostly cobbles or wood for a small toolbar. There must be NO UI, labels, readable signs, signs with lettering, borders, floating glyphs or panels. This image will fill a screen. Use believable proportions, friendly people large enough to interact with.

## Materials from the walkable harbor prototype

The original walkable prototype used ImageGen material studies. A panoramic daylight cloud sea in peach, teal, and lilac surrounds the floating terraces. A separate nocturnal study supplies the moon, cloud shadows, and stars after the beacon is rekindled. The sky and the harbor geometry appear in the local reflection probe.

ImageGen produced two orthographic atlases for the four ports. One contains Sunstep’s pear-crested guild doorway, the Furnace Choir’s stained glass, Copperfall’s riveted clock, and Meridian’s brass star dial. Each landmark has a color texture and a corresponding relief texture on its modeled wall. The other contains wisteria-strewn cobbles, the glassworkers’ violet paving, weathered timber slips, and the astronomers’ radial inlay. A separate material study supplies the small-scale pigment of limestone, wood, verdigris, and linen. The props and crews on the docks occupy the same 3D space as the captain.

## Working interiors and character materials

Sixteen ImageGen cabinet studies give each market, public office, shipyard and local institution its own close-range display. They depict the local job from the front so the painted shelves, tools and samples sit inside modeled cabinet joinery. Sunstep’s weighing room holds fruit and folded scions. A separate ImageGen sheet supplies sixteen front-facing character materials in the four cities. Facial pigment follows the brow, cheek and nose geometry on the animated head. The same worker uses the same face on the quay and indoors.

## Flying into port

Four transparent skyport illustrations from the original ImageGen island sheet mark the towns at a distance. Each fades into its corresponding Blender model on approach. The ships, people, local terrain, roads, jetties, lights, and service entrances share one set of world coordinates. The clear or squalling sky and the position of the sun determine the lighting.

[Build the four GLBs](../tools/ports/build_ports.py) with Blender in background mode. The recipes include Sunstep’s working mill, Copperfall’s open ship frame, Glasshouse’s ribbed barrel-vault conservatories and Meridian’s observatory. Meadow, limestone, strata and masonry use four textures cut from an original painted ground atlas generated for Skyrates. Each GLB carries named anchors for the five counters, their berths and their staff. The distant illustrations also serve captains whose devices cannot render the close-up models.
